Colorado NHA Licensure

This guide walks administrators through each of the three realistic Colorado NHA licensure pathways. Each pathway tab contains step-by-step instructions, required documents, fill-in templates, and auto-generated printable documents.

⚡ The Philosophy Behind This Guide

When an application lands at DORA, it is first reviewed by an entry-level staff member. That person makes one of two decisions:

  • Push it through — approve the application for examination. This is the goal.
  • Send to board review — this creates months of delay and real risk of denial.

A reviewer sends to board review any time they see something missing, unclear, or questionable. Our goal is to make every application so complete and so clear that the reviewer has no legitimate reason to escalate it. Every document maps directly to a rule requirement. When a reviewer reads our application, every question they could ask is already answered in front of them. That is why our applicants get pushed through.

The Three Pathways

Pathway 1
Master's Degree
Hold a qualifying Master's degree → Submit application → Board approves to test → Pass NAB national exam* → Pass CO state exam → License issued.
No experience required. Most direct route.
Pathway 2
ANHA Experience (Rule 1.7C)
BBA or Public Admin degree + 1 year as ANHA → Submit application → Board approves to test → Pass NAB* → Pass CO state exam → License issued.
Most common path for our admins.
Pathway 3
Endorsement
Active NHA license actively used for 12 consecutive months → Submit application → Pass CO state exam → License issued.
License must be actively held AND used.
* About the NAB National Exam NAB is not a prerequisite to apply in Colorado. If you have already passed the NAB Core and NHA modules — for example, through another state's licensure process — include your official NAB score reports with your application and note it in your Application Overview. If you have not yet taken NAB, that is completely fine: Colorado will authorize you to sit for NAB (and the Colorado state exam) once your application is approved by the Board.

Pathway 1 — Master's Degree

Under Rule 1.7(B), any applicant who holds a qualifying Master's degree may apply to sit for the Colorado NHA examination immediately. No work experience is required.

📘 NAB Timing The NAB national exam (Core + NHA modules) is not a prerequisite to applying. Colorado will authorize you to schedule NAB after the Board approves your application. If you have already passed NAB in another state, include your official score reports with your application — they satisfy Rule 1.6 and nothing further is needed on the national exam.
✓ Direct Route A Master's degree in Business Administration (MBA) or Management alone qualifies an applicant to sit for examination. No experience, no AIT, no additional requirements beyond the degree. This is the fastest path to licensure eligibility.

Step-by-Step Process

1
Hold a Qualifying Master's Degree
From a regionally accredited institution recognized by the U.S. Department of Education.
2
Submit Application to DORA
Complete the online application at apps2.colorado.gov/dora/licensing. Upload all required documents including the Application Overview and official transcripts. NAB score reports are only required at this stage if you have already passed NAB through another state. If you have not yet taken NAB, you will be authorized to schedule it after the Board approves your application.
3
Board Reviews and Approves
The Board reviews and grants approval to sit for examinations. All documents must be received within one year of the application date.
4
Pass NAB National Examination
Pass both the Core module and the NHA module of the NAB national examination. Most applicants take NAB after the Board approves them to test. If you have already passed NAB in another state, this step is already complete — your score reports satisfy Rule 1.6.
5
Pass Colorado State Examination
Pass the Colorado state exam covering state laws, facility licensure, building and fire safety, and NHA rules and regulations.
✓
License Issued
Colorado NHA license issued upon passing both examinations.

Qualifying Degrees — No Experience Required

Primary — MBA or Master's in ManagementMaster's degree in Management or Business Administration. Sit for exam immediately upon application approval. No experience required.

Additional degrees that also qualify under this pathway:

  • Bachelor's degree or higher in Public Health Administration or Health Administration
  • Bachelor's degree or higher in Health Care Management
  • Bachelor's degree or higher in Nursing Home Administration
  • Health Services Executive (HSE) qualification
⚠ AIT Note Colorado offers an AIT program as an alternative qualifying pathway. However the AIT must be formally approved by the Colorado Board before it begins, supervised by a Colorado-licensed NHA preceptor, and completed entirely within Colorado. Out-of-state AIT programs are not accepted under any circumstance. Due to the complexity, length, and risk of the AIT process, it is not featured in this guide.

Pathway 2 — ANHA Experience (Rule 1.7C)

Under Rule 1.7(C), an applicant who holds a Bachelor's degree in Business or Public Administration AND has completed one year of qualifying administrative experience as an Assistant Nursing Home Administrator may apply for the Colorado NHA examination.

📘 NAB Timing The NAB national exam is not a prerequisite to submitting your application. Colorado will authorize you to schedule NAB once the Board approves you to test. If you have already passed NAB through another state's licensure process, include your official score reports — they satisfy Rule 1.6.
🚨 Title Requirement Under the Rule 1.7(C) pathway, the only job title that will be used and recognized is Assistant Nursing Home Administrator (ANHA). Do not use any other title on your verification forms, job descriptions, or overview document. Any other title will invite scrutiny and risk board review or denial.

⚡ Why Documentation Is Everything on This Pathway

The Colorado Board reviews experience under Rule 1.7(C) with extreme scrutiny. Their default posture is to look for reasons to question whether the experience qualifies. An entry-level reviewer who sees anything unclear, missing, or ambiguous will escalate to full board review — adding months to the process and creating real risk of denial.

  • Every document explicitly addresses a specific rule requirement. The reviewer does not have to interpret anything.
  • The Application Overview maps each requirement to the evidence. The reviewer sees everything answered before them.
  • The job description documents all three required elements of Rule 1.7(E). If any element is missing, the experience will be questioned.
  • The Verification of Experience Form must be signed by a Licensed NHA — not just a supervisor.
  • When the packet is complete, a reviewer should be able to approve it in under five minutes without any escalation.

Experience Requirements — Rule 1.7(E) and 1.7(F)

Qualifying experience must include all three of the following elements. If any element is missing from the documentation, the Board will question whether the experience qualifies.

  • Rule 1.7(E)(1) — Day-to-day, on-site supervision of subordinatesDirect responsibility for supervising, directing, managing, monitoring, or exercising reasonable control over subordinates for one year. The number of direct reports must be documented.
  • Rule 1.7(E)(2) — Coordination of interdisciplinary patient careCoordination of interdisciplinary patient care and services across nursing, rehab, dietary, social services, and other departments.
  • Rule 1.7(E)(3) — Planning, finance, budget, and policy involvementPlanning, organizing, and controlling events toward a goal — including involvement in finance, budget, and policy-making decisions.
🚫 Excluded Duties — Rule 1.7(F) Experience does NOT include: housekeeping, laundry, food service, activities, medical records, bookkeeping, or clerical responsibilities. These do not count toward the one-year requirement even if performed at a qualifying facility. Confirm in writing that the role contained none of these duties.

Who Should Sign the Verification Forms

📌 Signing Authority — Follow This Order First choice: If your previous employer had a Licensed NHA as your preceptor or direct supervisor, and that person is willing to sign the Verification of Experience Form — use them. This is the most straightforward path.

If unavailable: If your previous employer's Licensed NHA is unwilling or unavailable to sign, contact your Cottonwood regional administrator for guidance on the appropriate signing authority for your specific situation.

The Verification of Experience Form must be signed by a Licensed Nursing Home Administrator. The rule does not require the signing NHA to be licensed in Colorado — a licensed NHA from any state is acceptable.

Pathway 3 — Endorsement

Under Rule 1.8, an applicant who holds an active NHA license in another state may apply for Colorado licensure by endorsement through the Occupational Credential Portability Program.

🚨 Critical — Read This First The applicant must have held an active, unrestricted NHA license AND actively practiced as a licensed nursing home administrator for one full consecutive year — 12 months — immediately preceding the date the application is received. Both conditions must be met for the entire 12-month period. A license held but not actively practiced under does not satisfy this requirement. There are no exceptions.

Step-by-Step Process

1
Hold a Current, Active, Unrestricted NHA License
Active in another state or territory at the time of application. The license must be unrestricted and in good standing.
2
Actively Practice for 12 Consecutive Months
Actively practice as a licensed nursing home administrator for 12 consecutive months immediately preceding the application date. The license must be actively held AND actively used in practice. This 12-month window must end immediately before application — a prior period followed by a gap does not qualify.
3
Submit Application to DORA
Complete the online application with all required documents and the application fee.
4
Board Reviews and Approves
The Board reviews and grants approval to sit for the Colorado state examination.
5
Pass Colorado State Examination
Pass the Colorado state exam. The NAB national exam requirement is satisfied by the exam passed in the originating state.
✓
License Issued
Colorado NHA license issued upon passing the Colorado state examination.

All Four Eligibility Requirements — All Must Be Met

Requirement 1 — Active License

Must hold a current, active, and unrestricted NHA license in good standing in another state or territory at the time of application.

Requirement 2 — 12 Consecutive Months of Active Practice

Must have actively practiced as a licensed nursing home administrator for 12 consecutive months immediately preceding the application date. The license must be actively held AND actively used in practice for the entire period. Holding a license without practicing under it does not qualify. The 12 months must end immediately before the application date — not a prior period.

Requirement 3 — National Examination

Must have passed the NAB national examination in the state where currently licensed.

Requirement 4 — Colorado State Examination

Must schedule and pass the Colorado state examination within one year of being approved to sit.

🚫 Does Not Count Toward 12 Months Experience as an Assistant Nursing Home Administrator, Administrator-in-Training, or in any role under the supervision of another licensed NHA does not satisfy the 12-month active practice requirement. The applicant must have held and actively used their own NHA license as the licensed administrator of record for the full 12 consecutive months.
